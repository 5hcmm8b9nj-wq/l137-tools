Understand It: What Is a Strongman Spirit?

Simple Explanation

In the Bible, a "strongman" is pictured as a heavily armed guard who holds a house and its goods captive so nothing can be taken from it — until someone stronger overpowers him. Deliverance teaching uses this picture for a spiritual force that tries to hold a person's mind, finances, home, destiny, or family line in bondage. The good news of this Prophetic Direction is that Jesus Christ is the "stronger than he" of Luke 11:22 — He has already overcome, and every prayer below is prayed from that finished victory, not from fear.

The Scriptures Behind This Direction

"When a strong man armed keepeth his palace, his goods are in peace: But when a stronger than he shall come upon him, and overcome him, he taketh from him all his armour wherein he trusted, and divideth his spoils."

Luke 11:21-22 KJV
What does this mean?
Jesus spoke these words to describe how a strongman keeps goods "in peace" only until someone stronger defeats him and takes back what was stolen. Jesus is that Stronger One. He has already defeated every strongman through the cross and resurrection (Colossians 2:15). This Scripture is the legal ground for the prayers that follow — you are not fighting to become strong; you are standing on a victory Christ already won.
Deeper Study: Cross-Referenced with Deliverance Teachers

Frank and Ida Mae Hammond, in their foundational deliverance text Pigs in the Parlor, ground the whole strongman picture in this same passage, noting that the Greek word behind "his armour" (panoplia) is the same word Paul uses in Ephesians 6:11 for the believer's own armor. Their conclusion: once stripped, the strongman is left completely defenseless. "It is imperative that we recognize Satan to be a defeated foe. He is stripped of his power and his kingdom. We have every right to treat him as a trespasser" (Hammond, Pigs in the Parlor, ch. 2).

"O earth, earth, earth, hear the word of the LORD. Thus saith the LORD, Write ye this man childless, a man that shall not prosper in his days: for no man of his seed shall prosper, sitting upon the throne of David, and ruling any more in Judah."

Jeremiah 22:29-30 KJV
What does this mean?
God told the prophet Jeremiah to speak directly to the earth as a witness against a wicked king's line. Deliverance teaching draws from this pattern — addressing the earth as a witness — as the biblical basis for the "O earth, earth, earth" cry used in the prayers below, calling creation itself to bear witness to God's word over your life. The authority behind the cry is still the word and the blood of Jesus, not the earth itself.
Deeper Study: Cross-Referenced with Deliverance Teachers

Worth noting plainly: the specific "earth, earth, earth" address used in this study is not found word-for-word in Derek Prince, the Hammonds, John Eckhardt, Dr. Olukoya, Don Dickerman, or Archbishop Duncan-Williams. It appears to be this teaching's own distinctive application of the Jeremiah 22:29 pattern rather than a phrase borrowed from a published tradition — good to know plainly rather than assume a precedent that isn't there.

"For the life of the flesh is in the blood: and I have given it to you upon the altar to make an atonement for your souls: for it is the blood that maketh an atonement for the soul."

Leviticus 17:11 KJV
What does this mean?
Under the Old Covenant, blood on the altar made atonement — it dealt with sin and opened the way back to God. That entire pattern pointed forward to Jesus. Hebrews 9:12 says Christ entered "by his own blood" to obtain "eternal redemption." When this direction says "the blood of Jesus," it is pointing to His finished, once-for-all atonement — the communion wine represents and points to that blood; the wine itself has no power apart from what it represents.
Deeper Study: Cross-Referenced with Deliverance Teachers

Frank and Ida Mae Hammond teach that the blood's benefit must be personally, deliberately applied by faith, not merely acknowledged: "The blood must be personally applied. Every man who applies the blood by faith is saved. Those who refuse or neglect to apply that blood are lost" (Hammond, Pigs in the Parlor, ch. 15). Archbishop Nicholas Duncan-Williams teaches the same ground of authority: "Claim victory by the blood of Jesus over the strong man" (Duncan-Williams, Binding the Strong Man).

The Instruction

TAKE YOUR BLOOD OF JESUS, GO OUTSIDE AND STAND ON THE EARTH, PRAY THIS PRAYERS AND POUR YOUR COMMUNION WINE 🍷 ON THE GROUND (EARTH)
Why this instruction?
Standing on the earth — you are speaking God's word over the very ground your life, home, and inheritance stand on, following the Jeremiah 22:29 pattern of addressing the earth as a witness.

Pouring communion wine on the ground — the wine is a physical, remembered symbol of Christ's blood (Matthew 26:27-28). Pouring it out is a visible act of faith declaring that the atonement of Leviticus 17:11 has already been fulfilled by Jesus, and that His blood — not the wine itself — is what silences and binds every strongman named in the prayers.

The prayers — every declaration you speak is anchored "by the blood of Jesus" and "in Jesus' name," keeping the authority on Christ and His finished work throughout.
Deeper Study: Cross-Referenced with Deliverance Teachers

Every teacher researched for this study affirms that the blood of Jesus — never a physical substance on its own — is the legal ground for authority over the strongman. Think of pleading the blood the way the Hammonds describe it: not a magic phrase repeated for effect, but a personal, faith-filled appeal to what the blood has already accomplished, the same way you would personally endorse a check rather than just look at it (Hammond, Pigs in the Parlor, ch. 15).

    What this means: You open by naming the source of the power you are relying on — not your own strength, not the earth's power, but the Holy Ghost.     Archbishop Nicholas Duncan-Williams teaches that binding and loosing operates only "by the grace and mercy [of God], through the spilled blood of our Lord Jesus" — never by human strength (Duncan-Williams, Binding the Strong Man). This opening line names the Holy Ghost, not the earth itself, as that source of power.
