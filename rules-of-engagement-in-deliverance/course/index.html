The order of engagement

Before the eleven rules, this is the order every deliverance session should follow. Never skip a step and never reorder them.

1
Praise and Worship
You do not begin a battle by talking about the battle. You begin by magnifying the One who fights it. Psalm 22:3
2
Thanksgiving
Thanksgiving settles the heart and reminds you of every victory God has already given. Psalm 149:5-9
3
Confession of Sins
Unconfessed sin is legal ground. Confession removes it before a single word is spoken to the enemy. 1 John 1:7-9
4
Forgive Others
Unforgiveness is the one door the Lord Himself tells us to shut before we pray. Mark 11:24-26
5
Invite the Holy Spirit
Deliverance is not a technique. It is the Spirit of God working through a yielded believer. Zechariah 4:6
6
Teaching
People must understand what is happening to them. Understanding is part of the deliverance. Hebrews 4:12
7
Deliverance Prayers and Declarations
Now you speak — with authority, in the name of Jesus, against what the Word has exposed. Mark 16:15-18
8
Sealing
Cover everything that has been cleansed with the blood of Jesus and fill the house. Revelation 12:10-11

Rule 1 of 11

Know Your Enemy and Know the Battlefield

Never fight a spiritual war with human weapons.

Understand it

What this means

The real fight is not with people. It is with unseen spirits. So we do not fight with our fists or our words against people. We fight with prayer, the Bible, and the name of Jesus.

The person who insulted you is not your real enemy. Behind wrong behaviour there is often a spiritual influence pushing it. That is why arguing harder never fixes it. You have to fight on the level where the fight is actually happening.

Paul is precise: our wrestling is not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places. He names four ranks. That means the opposition is organised, not random. It also means every purely human strategy — arguing, avoiding, out-manoeuvring, cutting people off — will leave the actual source untouched.

Ephesians 6:12 lists principalities (archas — rulers), powers (exousias — authorities), rulers of the darkness of this world (kosmokratoras), and spiritual wickedness in high places (ta pneumatika tes ponerias en tois epouraniois). Paul writes to a church in Ephesus, a city dominated by occult practice, where Acts 19 records the burning of the books of curious arts. He is not being poetic. He is describing a real hierarchy. Second Corinthians 10 answers it with an equally precise weapon-set: weapons that are not carnal but mighty through God to the pulling down of strong holds, casting down imaginations and every high thing that exalteth itself against the knowledge of God.

Understand it

What this means

Jesus already beat the devil on the cross. So when we pray, we are not trying to win. We are telling the enemy about a fight he already lost.

Think of it like a match that is already finished and the score is final. You are not playing to win — you are enforcing a result. That changes how you stand, how you speak, and how afraid you are.

Colossians 2:15 says He spoiled principalities and powers, made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it. The victory is historic and finished. Ephesians 1 then says God raised Christ and set Him far above all principality and power and might and dominion, and Ephesians 2 says He hath raised us up together, and made us sit together in heavenly places in Christ Jesus. The believer's position is above, not below.

Colossians 2:15 uses the imagery of a Roman triumph — the victorious general parading disarmed enemies through the streets. Christ did this at the cross, having blotted out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us. Note the order in Ephesians 1:19-23 and 2:4-6: God's mighty power was wrought in Christ when He raised Him, then that same power seats the believer in Him. Luke 10 balances it — Jesus grants authority to tread on serpents and scorpions and over all the power of the enemy, yet immediately corrects the disciples' joy: rejoice not that the spirits are subject unto you, but rather rejoice, because your names are written in heaven. Authority is real, but identity in Christ is the greater ground of rejoicing.

No Borrowed Authority

You cannot use the name of Jesus without belonging to Jesus.

Understand it

What this means

Some men tried to use the name of Jesus, but they did not know Jesus. It went very badly for them. First belong to Jesus. Then you can use His name.

Borrowing someone's password does not make you the account owner. The sons of Sceva found that out. The demon literally said: Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye? Authority is not copied. It is given to people who are actually His.

Acts 19 records seven sons of Sceva, a Jewish chief priest, using the formula, We adjure you by Jesus whom Paul preacheth. The evil spirit answered, Jesus I know, and Paul I know; but who are ye? The man leaped on them and prevailed, so that they fled out of that house naked and wounded. The name of Jesus is not a formula. It is the authority of a Person, exercised by people who are in relationship with Him.

Notice what the demon recognised: Jesus (the source of authority) and Paul (the one under that authority). It recognised neither the office of the exorcists nor their religious pedigree — their father was a chief priest. Religion without relationship is exposed instantly in the spirit realm. Romans 10:9-10 gives the doorway: confess with thy mouth the Lord Jesus, and believe in thine heart that God hath raised him from the dead. James 4 then gives the posture: submit yourselves therefore to God. Resist the devil, and he will flee from you. Submission comes before resistance in that verse, and the order is not decorative.

Rule 4 of 11

Clean Hands — Close the Door Before You Command

Deal with legal ground first. Unconfessed sin and unforgiveness are open doors.

Understand it

What this means

If a door in your house is open, anything can walk in. Sin and unforgiveness are open doors. Say sorry to God, forgive the person who hurt you, and the door closes.

You cannot tell someone to get out of a room while you are holding the door open for them. Confession and forgiveness are how you shut the door. It is not weakness — it is closing the entry point.

Jesus said, the prince of this world cometh, and hath nothing in me (John 14:30). That is the standard: nothing for the enemy to hold on to. Ephesians 4:27 warns, neither give place to the devil — the word place is topos, a legal foothold. First John 1:9 provides the remedy: if we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins, and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness. And Mark 11:25 makes forgiveness a precondition of prayer itself: when ye stand praying, forgive, if ye have ought against any.

Three distinct doors appear in these passages. First, unconfessed sin — answered by 1 John 1:7-9, where the blood of Jesus Christ his Son cleanseth us from all sin. Second, unresolved anger — Ephesians 4:26-27 ties let not the sun go down upon your wrath directly to neither give place to the devil, meaning prolonged anger creates topos. Third, unforgiveness — Matthew 6:15 states plainly, but if ye forgive not men their trespasses, neither will your Father forgive your trespasses. Second Corinthians 2:11 names the reason this matters in warfare: lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices. Deliverance that skips this stage is treating symptoms while the legal claim stands.

Rule 5 of 11

Never Step Onto the Field Undressed

The whole armour — not a favourite piece of it.
✓ Lesson complete

Ephesians 6:10-18 (KJV)

Ephesians 6:10Finally, my brethren, be strong in the Lord, and in the power of his might.

Ephesians 6:11Put on the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil.

Ephesians 6:12For we wrestle not against flesh and blood, but against principalities, against powers, against the rulers of the darkness of this world, against spiritual wickedness in high places.

Ephesians 6:13Wherefore take unto you the whole armour of God, that ye may be able to withstand in the evil day, and having done all, to stand.

Ephesians 6:14Stand therefore, having your loins girt about with truth, and having on the breastplate of righteousness;

Ephesians 6:15And your feet shod with the preparation of the gospel of peace;

Ephesians 6:16Above all, taking the shield of faith, wherewith ye shall be able to quench all the fiery darts of the wicked.

Ephesians 6:17And take the helmet of salvation, and the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God:

Ephesians 6:18Praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, and watching thereunto with all perseverance and supplication for all saints;

Understand it

What this means

God gave us armour — like a soldier's clothes. We put on all of it, not just one piece. Then we can stand and not fall.

Nobody plays a contact sport in one shin pad. Paul lists six pieces plus prayer. Skipping one is not a style choice — it is an exposed spot.

Twice Paul says the whole armour of God — verse 11 and verse 13 — and both times the stated purpose is to stand: that ye may be able to stand against the wiles of the devil, and having done all, to stand. Truth, righteousness, the preparation of the gospel of peace, faith, salvation, the sword of the Spirit, and praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit. Six pieces and a posture.

Note what the armour is for. Paul does not describe a charge; he describes a stand — histemi, to hold ground already taken. Five pieces are defensive; only the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God (rhema, the spoken word), is offensive. And the list does not end at verse 17. Verse 18 continues the same sentence: praying always with all prayer and supplication in the Spirit, and watching thereunto with all perseverance and supplication for all saints. Prayer is not an addition to the armour — it is how the armour is worn.

Understand it

What this means

Not every spiritual thing comes from God. The Bible says to test it. And not every problem is a demon — sometimes it is a habit, a hurt, or a health need.

Discernment is not suspicion. It is checking. The Bible literally tells you to try the spirits whether they are of God. That protects you from being fooled in both directions — seeing demons everywhere, or refusing to see them at all.

First John 4:1 commands: believe not every spirit, but try the spirits whether they are of God: because many false prophets are gone out into the world. First Corinthians 14:33 anchors the atmosphere: God is not the author of confusion, but of peace — and verse 40, let all things be done decently and in order. Confusion, pressure, and disorder are diagnostics, not credentials.

The test in 1 John 4:2-3 is Christological: every spirit that confesseth that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is of God, and every spirit that confesseth not that Jesus Christ is come in the flesh is not of God. The doctrine of the incarnation is the dividing line, not the impressiveness of the manifestation. Second Corinthians 2:11 adds the discipline of study: lest Satan should get an advantage of us: for we are not ignorant of his devices. Discernment therefore has two edges — it refuses to credit the enemy with what is merely flesh, weakness, illness, or unrenewed thinking, and it refuses to excuse as ordinary what is genuinely spiritual. Where mental or physical health may be involved, qualified pastoral and medical care belong alongside prayer, not in competition with it.

Understand it

What this means

When something wrong leaves, fill the space with God. Read the Bible. Pray. Worship. Stay with God's people. Do not leave the room empty.

Jesus described a house swept and garnished — clean, but empty. Empty is the problem. Freedom has to be filled with new habits or the old ones come back with company.

Matthew 12:43-45 describes the unclean spirit returning to find the house empty, swept, and garnished — and then bringing seven other spirits more wicked than himself, so the last state of that man is worse than the first. Romans 12:2 gives the filling: be not conformed to this world: but be ye transformed by the renewing of your mind. John 8:31-32 gives the anchor: if ye continue in my word, then are ye my disciples indeed; and ye shall know the truth, and the truth shall make you free. Galatians 5:1 gives the guard: stand fast therefore in the liberty wherewith Christ hath made us free.

The word garnished means decorated — the house was not only clean, it looked good. Appearance is not occupancy. The remedy in Scripture is occupancy: continuing in His word (John 8:31), renewing the mind (Romans 12:2), meditating in the book of the law day and night (Joshua 1:8), and standing fast in liberty (Galatians 5:1). Isaiah 10:27 names what breaks yokes — the anointing. Romans 8:37-39 seals the whole study: in all these things we are more than conquerors through him that loved us, and nothing shall be able to separate us from the love of God, which is in Christ Jesus our Lord. And Luke 1:37 remains the ground of every prayer prayed in this study: For with God nothing shall be impossible.

Repentance

Father, I come before You in the name of Jesus Christ.

I confess every sin known and unknown, and I ask You to cleanse me from all unrighteousness.

Wash me in the blood of Jesus and create in me a clean heart.

Forgiveness

Lord, I forgive every person who has wronged, wounded, betrayed, or rejected me.

I release them fully, and I ask You to bless them.

I forgive myself, and I receive Your forgiveness over my own failures.

Renunciation

I renounce every agreement I have made with the enemy, knowingly or unknowingly.

I renounce every word of fear, every vow of bitterness, and every ungodly covenant.

I renounce every occult contact and every practice that dishonours Christ.

Closing Legal Doors

I close every door that sin, anger, and unforgiveness have opened in my life.

I give no place to the devil, in the name of Jesus.

Let the enemy find nothing in me, as he found nothing in my Lord.

I put on the whole armour of God this day.

Truth around my life, righteousness over my heart, peace under my feet.

The shield of faith in my hand, the helmet of salvation on my mind, the sword of the Spirit in my mouth.

In the name of Jesus Christ, I command every unclean spirit assigned against my life to go.

Heal what the enemy has damaged in my body, my mind, and my emotions.

Restore the years and rebuild what was broken, in Jesus' name.

    Summary

    Deliverance is not a technique and it is not a performance. It is the believer, saved and submitted, standing in the finished victory of Christ, with clean hands and the whole armour on, using the weapons God actually gave — the blood, the Word, the name of Jesus and praise — speaking with authority rather than volume, discerning before engaging, going deeper in prayer and fasting when a thing does not move, refusing to rail or panic or act outside order, and then filling the house so the ground is held.

    The eleven rules at a glance

    1
    Know Your Enemy and Know the Battlefield
    Never fight a spiritual war with human weapons.
    2
    Fight From Victory, Not For Victory
    You engage from a seated position — the war was won at the cross.
    3
    No Borrowed Authority
    You cannot use the name of Jesus without belonging to Jesus.
    4
    Clean Hands — Close the Door Before You Command
    Deal with legal ground first. Unconfessed sin and unforgiveness are open doors.
    5
    Never Step Onto the Field Undressed
    The whole armour — not a favourite piece of it.
    6
    The Weapons That Actually Work
    The blood, the Word, the name, and praise — in that confidence.
    7
    Speak To It — Authority, Not Volume
    Deliverance is commanded, not begged. But the authority is Christ's, never yours.
    8
    Discern Before You Engage
    Test the spirits. Not everything spiritual is from God, and not everything wrong is a demon.
    9
    Some Kinds Require Prayer and Fasting
    Not every fight yields at the same level. Some ground is taken on your knees over time.
    10
    Rules of Restraint — Never Rail, Never Fear, Never Freelance
    Even Michael did not bring a railing accusation. Stay under authority and in order.
    11
    Hold the Ground — Fill the House
    Deliverance is not the end. An empty house invites a worse return.
