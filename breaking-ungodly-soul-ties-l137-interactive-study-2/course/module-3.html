Module 3 of 8 · Breaking Ungodly Soul Ties

How Ungodly Soul Ties Are Formed

Five doorways named in the teaching — the answer to “how did I get here?”

Read it · Understand it

The teaching

How does a bad rope get tied? Pastor Kiky named five ways:

  1. Wrong sex — outside God's plan.
  2. Being hurt again and again by someone and staying.
  3. Not letting go of an old boyfriend or girlfriend, even inside.
  4. A big hurt (trauma) that keeps you stuck.
  5. Wrong agreements — words, promises, or rituals.

The question the room was asking: how did I even get here? Five ways an ungodly soul tie forms:

  1. Sex outside God's will — it is not just physical; it is spiritual. Not sexually transmitted disease — sexually transmitted demons.
  2. Toxic and abusive relationships — control, manipulation, emotional dependency and fear.
  3. Past relationships never released — the person left, but you did not.
  4. Trauma and deep emotional bonds — pain that ties you to a person or a moment.
  5. Covenants and spiritual agreements — words spoken, promises, ungodly dedications, blood covenants.

Pastor Kiky answered the question the room was asking: how did I get here? How did this ungodly soul tie form in my life? What did I do for me to be in this kind of soul tie? He named five doorways.

(a) Sexual relationships outside the will of God

Sex is not just physical, beloved; it is spiritual. Some sexual contact is not sexually transmitted disease, but sexually transmitted demons. A one-flesh bond is formed (1 Corinthians 6:16). If the other person is a satanic agent, a marine agent, a wizard, they can spiritually manipulate you, spiritually visit you, spiritually control you. They can destroy your potential, take your abilities, redirect your steps.

(b) Toxic and abusive relationships

Relationships marked by control, by manipulation, by emotional dependency and fear create unhealthy attachments that are difficult to break. The woman cannot leave the man who is battering her; her eyes are swollen and she is still sitting there. Why? Because she is one flesh with him. The bond will not be talked out — it must be broken.

(c) Past relationships that were never released

Some people have left physically, but are still connected emotionally and spiritually. Even in a new relationship, they must think of the old one before they can have contact with the new one. Heartbreak that cannot eat, cannot speak, is not itself for one week — because they are still spiritually and emotionally attached.

(d) Trauma and deep emotional bonds

Painful experience can tie you to people or to moments that continue to influence your life.

(e) Covenants and spiritual agreements

Some ties are strengthened by words spoken, by promises made, by ritual, by ungodly dedications, by blood covenants. Some relationships go deeper than the sex — an agreement is made.

Each of the five doorways matches a scriptural category. Doorway (a) sits on 1 Corinthians 6:16 and its one-flesh grammar. Doorway (b) is the fear-and-domination bond that the fruit of the Spirit (Galatians 5) directly opposes. Doorway (c) is unfinished repentance; the person has walked away from the sin but has not renounced the covenant beneath it. Doorway (d) leans on the biblical treatment of the soul (Psalm 121:7) — the inner person needs preservation and healing. Doorway (e) is the most explicit — spoken words, promises, and rites carry weight in scripture from Numbers 30 to Ecclesiastes 5:4-5.

Deliverance therefore has to be as specific as the doorway. A blanket prayer does not close a specific door.
