Module 6 of 8 · Breaking Ungodly Soul Ties

Freedom Through the Son

Jesus did not only come to forgive sins — He came to break every bondage.

Read it · Understand it

The teaching

Jesus came to do more than say sorry for us. He came to cut the ropes. He came to set us free.

God's word says: your bad agreement is cancelled. It cannot stand.

This is the pivot of the whole teaching. Modules 1-5 diagnosed the problem. Module 6 says: there is an answer, and His name is Jesus.

Jesus did not only come to forgive sins. He came to break every bondage. Isaiah 28:18 is God's own verdict over your ungodly covenant: it shall be disannulled. Not maybe. Shall.

You see, the good news is Jesus. Jesus did not only come to forgive sins; He came to break every bondage in our life and in our destiny. Every ungodly connection must bow to the power of the Holy Ghost. Every ungodly connection must bow to the power of the cross of Calvary, by the power of the Holy Ghost.

The covenant with death is disannulled

Isaiah 28:18 is the main scripture of this teaching. It says, “Your covenant with death shall be disannulled.” Some — or all — of your ungodly soul ties are covenants with death, because some of these ties destroy your life, mislead you, and take your original glory and your potential from your life. And your covenant with death shall be — what? — disannulled.

The legal ground

Christ hath redeemed us from the curse of the law, being made a curse for us (Galatians 3:13). Deliverance stands on His finished work, not our striving.

Isaiah 28 was written into Judah's political corruption — the leaders had made a covenant with death to shelter them from Assyria (v. 15). Verse 18 is God's judgment on that logic. The pastoral application here is not fanciful: an ungodly soul tie is exactly the personal counterpart of Judah's political covenant with death — a lying refuge that promises safety and delivers destruction. And God's verdict is the same in both cases: disannulled.

Galatians 3:13-14 supplies the mechanism. He was made a curse; we are redeemed from the curse; the blessing of Abraham and the promise of the Spirit come to us through Jesus Christ. John 8:36 supplies the personal promise: real freedom, not partial freedom.

Knowledge check

Scenario — what would you do?

A believer says, "I confessed the sin, but I still feel bound to my ex. Prayer isn't working." What does this teaching answer?

Outcome

You take Isaiah 28:18 as God's verdict, apply the blood of Jesus, and renounce the covenant out loud. Confession opened the way; renunciation is what breaks the tie.

Best response: Confession forgives the sin, but the covenant beneath it must also be broken by the blood — freedom is voice-activated (Rev 12:11).. Many people are praying, many are fasting, yet remain bound — not because God has not answered, but because an ungodly covenant still stands and has not been broken by the blood and the spoken word.

Apply it

Application question

PAUSE AND REFLECT

Read Isaiah 28:18 out loud right now, with your own soul-tie situation in mind. Say it slowly, three times. Then say John 8:36 out loud. Write down what shifted.
