## Preparation flow

1. Praise & worship: Turn your attention to the Lord, rather than to fear.
2. Thanksgiving: Name what you are grateful for before making requests.
3. Confession of sins: Confess your own sins and ask God for mercy.
4. Forgive others: Release bitterness without excusing harm or abandoning healthy boundaries.
5. Invite the Holy Spirit: Ask for help, wisdom, strength, and guidance.
6. Teaching: Read the Bible passage and distinguish its words from commentary.
7. Prayers & declarations: Pray in Jesus’ name and repeat the clear corporate prayer excerpts.
8. Sealing: Close in thanksgiving, entrusting yourself to Christ’s care and covering.

## Lesson 1: Begin with God, not with fear

Enter the study through worship, gratitude, repentance, forgiveness, and dependence on the Holy Spirit.

### Plain-language explanation

Thank God. Tell Him honestly where you need forgiveness. Ask the Holy Spirit to help you pray. You do not need to begin by searching for enemies.

### Thanksgiving comes first

All three services include thanksgiving before the main deliverance teaching. The June service reads Psalm 92 as a corporate expression of praise.

### Repentance is personal

The services move into confession and a request for cleansing. Apply this to your own choices, words, and conduct rather than assuming someone else’s guilt.

### Help comes from the Holy Spirit

The May 11 service describes the Holy Spirit as helper, comforter, and strengthener. Read John 14:15–17 and Romans 8:26 in their own words.

### Follow the prayer order

The eight-stage guide follows the ministry’s saved preparation framework. It is a learning guide, not a claim that every recording contains a separate spoken section for each stage.

### Visual learning map

1. Gratitude: Remember God’s goodness
2. Repentance: Bring your own heart honestly
3. Dependence: Ask for the Spirit’s help
4. Prayer: Proceed with faith and peace

### Context

Psalm 92 is a song of thanksgiving. Psalm 51 expresses confession, while Jesus’ promise of the Comforter in John 14 is connected to love and obedience. Let the passages shape prayer rather than using them as a formula.

## Lesson 2: Understand “familiar spirits”

Examine the term in Scripture before studying how it is used in the sermon.

### Plain-language explanation

Seek God’s guidance rather than trying to get guidance from the dead or from mediums. Read Scripture carefully before accepting a spiritual message.

### Scripture’s wording

Isaiah 8:19 speaks of consulting those who have familiar spirits. Verse 20 directs attention to the law and the testimony.

### The sermon’s broader use

The May 11 teaching uses the term for spiritual opposition described as familiar with a family’s history, and connects it with monitoring, manipulation, inherited covenants, and repeated patterns. These are the teaching’s interpretations, not findings about a learner’s family.

### Repeated patterns

The service calls listeners to pray about harmful recurring patterns and seek a different future. A repeated difficulty does not, by itself, establish a spiritual cause.

### A practical response

Turn toward God, reject practices that conflict with your Christian faith, and seek trusted counsel. Avoid guessing who in a family is responsible for a problem.

### Visual learning map

1. Question: Where am I seeking guidance?
2. Scripture: Read the passage in context
3. Choice: Turn toward God
4. Practice: Prayer, obedience, wise counsel

### Context

Isaiah 8:19–20 contrasts seeking mediums with seeking God and His word. Isaiah 29:4 uses the image of a voice from the ground in a judgment passage. Neither passage should be converted into a symptom checklist.

## Lesson 3: Discernment & the authority of Jesus

Study the account of Paul and the woman with a spirit of divination without turning it into a method of judging people by appearances.

### Plain-language explanation

A message sounding impressive does not make it trustworthy. In Acts 16, Paul acts in the name of Jesus Christ, not in his own power.

### Read the whole account

Acts 16:16–18 describes a woman whose masters profit from soothsaying. She follows Paul and his companions, speaking about them.

### What Paul does

Paul speaks to the spirit and commands it to come out in the name of Jesus Christ. The passage identifies the spirit in this particular biblical event.

### The sermon’s lesson

The May 11 teaching uses this account to warn against deception and to emphasize Christ’s authority. Knowing personal information is not a reason to accept a person’s spiritual claims without examination.

### Discernment with restraint

Study the text, pray, and involve trustworthy pastoral leadership. This activity does not identify a present-day person as possessed or authorize confrontation.

### Visual learning map

1. Encounter: Paul and companions go to prayer
2. Following: The woman follows for many days
3. Command: Paul speaks in Jesus Christ’s name
4. Outcome: The spirit leaves the same hour

### Context

The passage includes exploitation: the woman’s masters gain money from her soothsaying. Keep both her humanity and the specific biblical narrative in view. The course checks understanding of the account, not a learner’s ability to detect spirits.

## Lesson 4: Redemption & the new covenant

Place the teaching about freedom within the passages on Christ’s redemption, His blood, and the new covenant.

### Plain-language explanation

The teaching directs faith toward Jesus and His work. Freedom is not something an object, a payment, or this course can manufacture.

### Christ’s redemption

The May 11 service reads Galatians 3:13–14 while teaching about freedom from curses. The passage speaks of Christ redeeming us from the curse of the law and the promise of the Spirit through faith.

### The new covenant

Hebrews 12:24 names Jesus as mediator of the new covenant. The service contrasts this with the covenants it describes in its deliverance teaching.

### Blood and testimony

All three services draw on Revelation 12:11 in prayer. Its complete wording includes faithful testimony and not loving one’s life unto death; keep that context rather than reducing it to material success.

### Keep interpretation separate

The sermon applies Isaiah 28:18 to renouncing evil agreements. In the passage, the covenant with death appears within a warning of judgment; the full verse includes the overflowing scourge. The application must not replace the wording.

## Lesson 5: Understand “monitoring spirits”

Compare the ministry’s terminology with the biblical passages used in the two monitoring-spirit services.

### Plain-language explanation

The sermons call believers to prayer, watchfulness, and trust in God. You can practice these without concluding that a person, animal, or object is watching you spiritually.

### The teaching’s definition

The May 10 and June 17 services use “monitoring spirits” for spiritual opposition said to observe, report, hinder, or delay a person’s progress. This is a ministry term and interpretive framework, not a diagnosis established by the course.

### The passages used

Daniel 2:22 emphasizes God’s knowledge of hidden things. Psalm 56:6 describes hostile people marking the psalmist’s steps, and Psalm 37:32–33 pairs hostility with confidence in the Lord.

### Watchfulness and faith

The services use 1 Peter 5:8 for vigilance. The next verse, included for context, calls for steadfast resistance in faith and remembers the sufferings of fellow believers.

### Familiar and monitoring: overlap

In the sermon framework, “familiar” emphasizes a claimed connection with personal or family history; “monitoring” emphasizes claimed observation and interference. The terms overlap in the services. The Bible passages do not provide a formal two-category diagnostic system.

### Visual learning map

1. Familiar: Family-history emphasis in the sermon
2. Overlap: Opposition and hindrance in the teaching
3. Monitoring: Observation emphasis in the sermon
4. Shared response: Prayer, Scripture, faith, wisdom

### Context

The study does not turn the sermon’s examples about animals, objects, dreams, tiredness, illness, or questions from relatives into evidence of surveillance. Jeremiah 29:11 is a promise within the message to the exiles; it is not a guarantee of immediate success in every plan.

## Lesson 6: Wisdom, privacy & peaceful boundaries

Apply the services’ emphasis on discretion through calm, practical decisions about what you share and with whom.

### Plain-language explanation

You may keep personal plans private while still receiving help from trustworthy people. Privacy does not require fear or accusations.

### Discretion is a recurring instruction

Both monitoring-spirit services advise listeners to be thoughtful about sharing unfinished plans, personal information, and social-media updates.

### Prayer and wise action

The May 10 service explicitly joins prayer with wisdom, knowledge, and action. For study practice, choose a boundary you can explain calmly.

### Keep needed support

Discretion should not isolate you from safe friends, family, qualified professionals, or pastoral support. Share information with the people who need it to help you.

### A measured response

If there is an actual privacy or safety concern, document observable facts and use appropriate reporting channels. An ordinary question, a delayed plan, or an unsettling feeling does not establish another person’s intentions.

### Visual learning map

1. Pause: Does this need to be public?
2. Choose: Who needs this information?
3. Share: Use a calm, clear boundary
4. Continue: Pray, work, and receive support

### Context

Psalm 121 expresses trust in God’s keeping. In 2 Kings 6:16–17, Elisha reassures his servant in a specific narrative. Use these passages for faith and reassurance, not to confirm suspicions about neighbors or relatives.

## Plain-Language Glossary

- **Familiar spirits:** In Isaiah 8:19, associated with consulting mediums. In this teaching, the term is also applied to claimed spiritual opposition connected with family history.
- **Monitoring spirits:** The ministry’s term for claimed spiritual observation and interference. Not a diagnosis that can be established from ordinary events or symptoms.
- **Discernment:** Careful spiritual judgment. In this study, it includes examining Scripture, separating facts from interpretation, and seeking trustworthy counsel.
- **Divination / soothsaying:** Seeking hidden knowledge through forbidden spiritual means; the Acts 16 account describes a spirit of divination.
- **Renunciation:** A deliberate rejection. The teaching applies it to practices and agreements it regards as contrary to faith in Christ.
- **Covenant:** A binding agreement. Hebrews 12:24 names Jesus as mediator of the new covenant.
- **Redemption:** Deliverance or release. Galatians 3:13 says Christ redeemed us from the curse of the law.
- **Stronghold:** In this sermon vocabulary, an entrenched area of opposition. The term does not identify a specific person or prove a medical condition has a spiritual cause.
- **Sealing:** The ministry’s language for closing prayer with trust in Christ’s covering and protection.
- **Declaration:** Words spoken aloud in faith and prayer. A declaration is distinct from a verified prediction or guaranteed outcome.
- **Transcript excerpt:** A selected portion of the supplied text, with its wording checked against that document. It has not been verified against the original recording.
- **Study explanation:** Added editorial help for understanding or applying the lesson. It is not a quotation from the pastor.
