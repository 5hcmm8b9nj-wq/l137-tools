Bible Character Studies

Seven biblical figures named in this teaching, their story, and the deliverance principle each one carries.

Pastor Kiky Ocansey · L137 Deliverance Ministry International

Bible character study

David — the harp that silenced a distressing spirit

When a distressing spirit came upon King Saul, David was sent for to play the harp and to sing the words of the Psalms over him. Scripture records that Saul was refreshed and was well, and the evil spirit departed from him. David did not argue with the spirit; he filled the atmosphere with the word of God set to worship. The same David later wrote that the Lord teaches his hands to war and his fingers to fight, and that God delivered him from his strong enemy.

Deliverance principle

Words are spirit and they are alive. When a believer speaks the Psalms aloud in the atmosphere, distressing spirits are silenced and pushed back. Worship and the spoken word are not decoration around deliverance — in David's case they were the deliverance.

Bible character study

King Saul and the woman with a familiar spirit at Endor

Saul had lost the voice of God, and instead of repenting he sought a woman who had a familiar spirit at Endor and asked her to bring up Samuel. Saul had himself put the mediums out of the land, and yet in his fear he went to one by night in disguise. What proceeded from that consultation was not comfort but a sentence of death, and Saul died the next day. Scripture is blunt about the cause: he enquired of one that had a familiar spirit rather than enquiring of the Lord.

Deliverance principle

Desperation is the doorway a familiar spirit waits for. When God seems silent, the remedy is repentance and the Holy Spirit, never a medium. The living have no business seeking to the dead, and every consultation costs more than it ever gives.

Bible character study

Manasseh — the king who dealt with a familiar spirit

Manasseh caused his children to pass through the fire in the valley of the son of Hinnom, observed times, used enchantments and witchcraft, and dealt with a familiar spirit and with wizards. Scripture summarises it in one sentence: he wrought much evil in the sight of the LORD, to provoke him to anger. His personal traffic with these spirits became a national defilement that took generations to undo. Pastor Kiky read this verse in the middle of the deliverance as the Lord's furious rebuke against every such enemy.

Deliverance principle

What one person opens, a household and a nation can inherit. Dealing with a familiar spirit is never private, and it provokes God's anger precisely because it destroys the people He loves.

Bible character study

Hezekiah — the king who turned his face to the wall

Hezekiah was sick unto death, and the prophet Isaiah brought him a word to set his house in order because he would die and not live. Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and prayed, reminding God how he had walked before Him in truth and with a perfect heart, and he wept sore. Before Isaiah had left the middle court, the word of the LORD came again: the prayer was heard, the tears were seen, and fifteen years were added to his life.

Deliverance principle

God reveals in order to redeem. A familiar spirit reveals a hard thing so that it will happen; God reveals it so that you will pray and it will not. As soon as you turn your face to the wall, God terminates the appointments of the wicked over your life.

Bible character study

Peter — freed from the prison by an angel

Peter was held in prison, bound with two chains, sleeping between two soldiers, with keepers before the door. The church prayed without ceasing. An angel of the Lord came, a light shined in the prison, the chains fell off his hands, and Peter walked out past guard after guard until the iron gate opened of its own accord. He himself did not know at first that it was real, so completely was it the work of God and not of man.

Deliverance principle

This is the pattern the congregation declared over their own deliverance: I walk out free with angelic assistance right now, and no one can stop me. Chains, padlocks and restricting devices are not obstacles to God — and the prayers of the church are what the angel was answering.

Bible character study

The woman of Revelation 12

A woman clothed with the sun travailed to bring forth a man child, and a great red dragon stood before her to devour the child as soon as it was born. The child was caught up unto God, and the woman fled into a wilderness place prepared of God, where she was nourished. The dragon, cast down to the earth, persecuted the woman and cast a flood out of his mouth after her, but the earth helped the woman and swallowed the flood. The dragon then went to make war with the remnant of her seed.

Deliverance principle

The dragon did not chase the woman for what she was but for what she carried — she carried a Saviour. The intensity of an attack is a measure of an assignment, not of weakness. And in the same chapter where the pursuit is recorded, so is the provision: God prepared her place, God nourished her there, and the earth itself helped her.

Bible character study

The Israelites warned in Deuteronomy 18

As Israel came into the land God had given them, they were commanded not to learn to do after the abominations of those nations. Nine practices were named — passing a son or daughter through the fire, divination, observing times, enchanting, witchcraft, charming, consulting with familiar spirits, wizardry and necromancy. All of them were called an abomination unto the LORD, and it was because of these very abominations that God drove those nations out before Israel. The same chapter goes on to promise a Prophet whom God would raise up, in whose mouth He would put His words.

Deliverance principle

God never forbade a lawful voice without providing one. Israel was told to refuse the diviner because God Himself would speak to them; the church has the Comforter who teaches all things. Every consultation with a familiar spirit is therefore a rejection of a provision God has already made.
