After Deliverance — Doors Are Being Shut

Every visitation of the Lord shuts a door the enemy opened. Do not quit because you cannot see the one you were focused on — keep going, and let salvation and the word secure what deliverance opened.

Pastor Kiky Ocansey · L137 Deliverance Ministry International

God shuts doors one at a time. Even when you cannot see the change you wanted, other doors were closed. Keep praying. And the most important thing is to belong to Jesus — that is what keeps you safe.

Deliverance is not one dramatic moment and done. Picture a house with a hundred unlocked doors. Every time you pray, God locks more of them — including ones you never knew were open. So do not quit at door seven because the door you cared about is number sixty.

Pastor Kiky's closing pastoral counsel protects people from two errors: quitting because the presenting issue has not visibly changed, and treating deliverance as a substitute for salvation and discipleship. The remedy for both is the same — remain in the process, be established in Christ, and let the word renew the mind so the old pattern is never picked up again.

The New Testament pattern is deliverance followed by discipleship: the Gerasene man delivered and then commissioned to tell his household (Mark 5:19-20), the believers at Ephesus burning their curious books after coming to faith (Acts 19:18-20). Romans 12:2 makes the renewing of the mind the mechanism of lasting change, and Ephesians 4:22-24 gives the sequence — put off the old, be renewed in the spirit of the mind, put on the new. Sealing in prayer and renewal in the word work together; neither replaces the other.

The teaching

Deliverance is progressive as well as decisive. If there are a hundred doors, they are shut one by one — including doors you did not know were open, doors that were causing sickness and infirmity.

So the temptation after a deliverance session is to measure everything by the one issue you came for. The instruction is to keep going, because other doors were shut while you prayed.

Salvation is the anchor. Romans 10:9 was read and the salvation prayer was prayed, because it makes no sense to be delivered and leave unsaved — it is Christ who gives the strength to resist these spirits, and He comes with His Holy Spirit.

2 Corinthians 5:17 secures the new standing: old things are passed away — old familiar spirits, old practices, old generational practices, old cultural practices — and they are not to be picked up again.

Ephesians 3:16-19 was prayed over the congregation: strengthened with might by His Spirit in the inner man, Christ dwelling in the heart by faith, rooted and grounded in love, filled with all the fullness of God.

1 Peter 2:9 and Romans 8:15 close the identity question: a chosen generation, a royal priesthood, called out of darkness into marvellous light, having received not the spirit of bondage again to fear but the Spirit of adoption whereby we cry, Abba, Father.

The service sealed every deliverance — whatever has left will not return, that door is completely closed — and dismissed the congregation with the grace and with peace.

Pray

  • Father, I seal my deliverance with the blood of Jesus — whatever has left me will never return, in the name of Jesus.
