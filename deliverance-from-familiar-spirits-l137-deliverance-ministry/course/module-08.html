God Reveals In Order To Redeem

A familiar spirit reveals in order for the evil to happen. God reveals so that you can pray and it does not happen. Hezekiah turned his face to the wall and the sentence was reversed.

Understand it · Choose your level

Simple, Teen, Adult or Deeper

God sometimes shows us hard things. He is not telling us it must happen. He is telling us to pray so it does not happen. Hezekiah prayed and God gave him fifteen more years.

Two voices can tell you the same bad news for opposite reasons. One tells you so it will land. The other tells you so you can stop it. Test the voice by its purpose: God reveals to redeem.

This module gives the congregation a discernment tool that is practical and immediate: examine the purpose of a revelation. Does it drive you to prayer and to God, or does it drive you to fear, dependence on a man, and fatalism? Pastor Kiky's use of Hezekiah establishes that a divine warning is an invitation to intercede, and her warning about one-directional prophets of death gives the negative test.

Compare Jonah's preaching to Nineveh (Jonah 3:4-10), where an unconditional-sounding word was answered by repentance and the judgement was not executed, with Amos 7:1-6, where the prophet's intercession stopped a declared judgement. Scripture therefore records a category of prophetic word that is announced precisely so that it may be averted. Hezekiah's case (2 Kings 20:1-6; Isaiah 38) is the clearest instance: the same prophet who delivered the sentence returned with the reprieve after prayer.

The teaching

Some false prophets only ever see death — the accident, the day you will die, the hospital rush. They never see prosperity and never see the glory of the Lord. That one-directional sight is a mark of the spirit behind them.

Hezekiah is the test case. The word came that he would die. He turned his face to the wall and prayed, and God sent the prophet back with fifteen added years. The revelation was real; the outcome was reversible.

So when God shows a hard thing, He is showing you the mindset of the enemy so that the church can gather in faith and pray it down — not announcing an unchangeable fate.

The familiar spirit's revelation has the opposite purpose: it is spoken in order to be fulfilled, and it aims at death.

Hosea 4:6 explains why revelation matters at all: people perish for lack of knowledge. What you do not know, you cannot resist. What God reveals, you can pray about.

Job 28:7 is used to describe a place in God beyond the sight of the birds of prey — “there is a path which no fowl knoweth, and which the vulture's eye hath not seen.” Get closer to God, and there is a place where defeat is not possible.
